Illustrative scenario · Not a standardized detector

When does a pattern merit a closer look?

A relay compares subscribers receiving the same live track under the same service policy and similar path conditions during a ten-minute window.

The comparison can flag behavior consistent with an advantage. It cannot prove AI use, unfair intent, or a policy violation.
Step 1 · Match the comparison group

Choose a fair denominator

Seven pseudonymous subscriptions share the same track, service class, ten-minute observation window, and comparable route quality. The example uses the peers’ median to reduce the effect of one unusually high or low subscriber.

Content

Same track and interval

Each subscriber had the same live objects available and was active for the full window.

Policy

Same service treatment

All had the same authorized service class and priority policy; no emergency or premium class is mixed into the cohort.

Network

Comparable path

Observed route and congestion indicators are similar. A material path difference would invalidate or split the comparison.

Step 3 · Interpret, then review

Example flag rule

This rule is only a teaching example. The proposal does not standardize a threshold or detector.

Flag for review when all three conditions hold:

Resource use exceeds 2× the matched cohort median; delivery delay is at least 25% lower; both conditions persist in at least 8 of 10 one-minute windows.

Subscriber A meets the example rule.

Egress is 2.4× the peer median, delay is 43% lower, and both are present in 9 of 10 windows. The elevated update count adds context about request behavior.

Output a bounded result.

“Sustained resource and outcome disparity observed in cohort C during window W; review measurement and local policy.” Do not label the endpoint as AI, abusive, or unfair.

Step 4 · Check alternative explanations

Before treating the signal as meaningful

Possible confounders

  • Different track variant, bitrate, object availability, or active duration.
  • Better route, lower access congestion, publisher-side timing, or a cache warm-up effect.
  • Legitimate policy or priority differences not represented in the cohort.
  • Measurement gaps, clock skew, or request counters that include retries.

Privacy handling

  • Use short-lived pseudonyms; retain aggregates where feasible.
  • Keep only fields needed for comparison and a limited review window.
  • Restrict access to drill-down evidence and record its use.
  • Do not inspect payload or infer subscriber identity from an opaque label.
What this example demonstrates: a reproducible cohort comparison and a review signal. It does not demonstrate causality, identify AI, set a universal fairness standard, or automatically throttle a subscriber. A relay DoS policy may independently enforce resource limits under its local rules.
