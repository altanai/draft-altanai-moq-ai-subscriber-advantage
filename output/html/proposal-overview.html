Individual proposal · Media over QUIC

Detecting patterns consistent with subscriber advantage

An IETF proposal to detect patterns consistent with an advantage, while limiting exposure of subscriber data.

The question is whether comparable subscribers show a sustained disparity in relay resource use or service outcomes. Observations alone cannot establish that AI was used or caused the disparity.
System view

Components and data flow

The relay observes requests and delivery conditions. A privacy guard limits what the comparison and review stages retain.

No payload inspection is proposed. A signal indicates a pattern for review; it does not identify an AI endpoint, prove intent, or prescribe enforcement.

What it adds

A comparative fairness lens

The contribution is a measurement question and privacy-aware review scope—not a new relay control protocol.

Measure in context

Compare like with like

Interpret repeated resource use and service outcomes among subscriptions with comparable content, service policy, time window, and relevant path conditions.

  • Resource signals: egress bytes, queue or buffer pressure, cache effects.
  • Outcome signals: join latency, object delivery delay, available quality indicators.
Keep the claim bounded

Indicator, not verdict

A sustained disparity is a reason to review measurement and policy. It does not show that AI caused the pattern or that a subscriber broke a rule.

  • No AI-versus-human classification.
  • No standardized algorithm, threshold, enforcement, or remediation.
  • Privacy controls should bound identity, exposure, and retention.
Background research

Related work and boundaries

These efforts address adjacent layers of the problem. The proposal should cite the relay DoS draft and distinguish its comparative measurement goal from its mitigations.

WorkWhat it coversRelationship to this proposal
MoQ Relay DoS -02Relay threat analysis and operational advice: resource cost tracking, per-client limits, rate limiting, and resource isolation/fairness policies.Strong overlap in signals and relay resource concerns. This proposal emphasizes sustained, cohort-based comparison of resource use and service outcomes, with privacy limits.
RFC 6357, 7339, 7415SIP overload design and signaling-rate controls; RFC 6357 also discusses allocating signaling capacity among upstream neighbors.Useful precedent for load protection and capacity sharing. Different layer and unit from subscriber-level MoQ relay outcome comparison.
RFC 4412; RFC 8888SIP resource-priority requests under local policy; RTCP feedback for media congestion control.Priority authorization and path congestion remain their own mechanisms; neither supplies the proposed MoQ comparison.
SIP AI-Session-ID -00Expired individual problem statement for a stable application-level identifier to correlate an AI voice workflow across SIP dialogs and components.Could support event correlation in a SIP system; it does not define resource measurement or fairness detection.
AIPREF privacy-control draft and MoQ metrics workAdjacent work on AI data-use preferences and MoQ measurement.Privacy preferences can bound telemetry exposure; measurement definitions can inform signals. Neither alone decides whether a disparity is an advantage.
Most useful next step: define a worked measurement example with a matched cohort, a sustained-disparity rule, plausible confounders, and a privacy-limited review output. Keep any threshold explicitly illustrative until the working group agrees on requirements.
